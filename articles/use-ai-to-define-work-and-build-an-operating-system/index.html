What is the AI actually doing? / 6. Making it work

"Can we get this working?" Build a business operating system with AI

You do not have to arrive with a perfectly specified task. You may know the business needs to work differently before you can describe the system it needs. Models can help you develop that understanding and turn it into something people can use.

Published by Good Remedy · Article

Use the model to help discover the work

One of the ideas in the founder's writing is that a system can examine a business, find the workflows or work toward a goal, and help construct the places where AI could contribute. That puts models to work before the automation has been chosen.

Start with a real ambition or difficulty. The company wants to serve more customers well. A delivery team keeps receiving incomplete work. An owner carries too much of the coordination personally. Ask the model to help examine what must happen, who participates and where the current arrangement becomes difficult.

Give it relevant evidence you are authorized to use: examples of the work, current procedures, the systems involved and accounts from the people doing the job. Let it propose explanations, identify missing information and ask useful questions. Test that proposed picture with those people. A plausible process diagram can still be wrong.

Build toward the operation you want

An existing process is evidence about the starting point. It does not have to be the limit of the design. The founder made this distinction directly: companies may want to apply AI to ordinary work or develop a better way of working, as well as repair an existing AI workflow.

Models can help compare alternatives. Would a shared view remove repeated status meetings? Should information be collected earlier? Does a handoff need a different owner? Which parts need interpretation, which need ordinary software and which depend on a human decision?

Keep the company's goal connected to the working day. Leadership needs to understand the direction and results. A department head needs a workable plan. The person doing the job needs enough information and authority to complete the next step. The design should make those levels support one another.

Turn the design into something people can use

An operating system for work means the arrangement of people, software, information, decisions and ongoing coordination through which the business actually functions. Here, it does not mean replacing Windows or buying one application that must do everything.

The deliverable could include a working interface, connected records, task assignments, exception handling and a reliable way to carry changes between teams. Models can assist with requirements, interface drafts, implementation code, test cases and operating instructions. Each of those outputs still needs to be checked against the work it is supposed to serve.

Begin with a complete, useful part of the larger operation. For customer onboarding, that might run from the accepted sale to the delivery team's acceptance of a service-ready customer. Understand what that passage depends on, then decide what the first release must contain.

Use existing systems where they fit. Confirm actual access and integration feasibility before promising a connection. Keep important permissions enforceable in the software that performs the action. A model's description of a control does not install the control.

Find out whether the operation works

Run representative work through the implementation. Try an incomplete input, a changed requirement and a handoff to a person who did not help design it. Inspect the result where it is supposed to arrive. Record what still requires manual attention.

Use what happens to improve the operation. Watch the time spent producing, reviewing, correcting and coordinating the work. A faster local step is useful when it contributes to a better completed result. Maintain the system as responsibilities, software and circumstances change.

This is the constructive direction of the series. Understanding drift, hallucination and misleading behavior helps us decide what conditions the work requires. Those conditions can then become part of how an operation is built and used.

Good Remedy helps form, implement and develop that arrangement. The ambition can span the business while the first delivery remains concrete enough to build, test and use. Start by asking what your people should be able to accomplish, then make the path to that result real.
