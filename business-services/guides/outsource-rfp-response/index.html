When does it make sense to outsource an RFP response?

RFP response work is a strong outsourcing candidate when the company has the source material and subject-matter owners but lacks enough proposal capacity to build the compliance matrix, chase missing answers and assemble the submission.

Published by Good Remedy · Business service guide

Outsource the coordination, not the truth

The outside operator can read the solicitation, map every requirement, assemble approved facts, draft narrative and track attachments. It should not invent past performance, certifications, staff qualifications or commercial terms to make the response look complete.

Start with the compliance matrix

A polished answer that misses a mandatory form or answers the wrong requirement is still a bad proposal. The first artifact should show what the buyer asked for, where the answer will come from and which items remain unresolved.

Make missing information visible early

The biggest time loss in proposal work is often not writing. It is discovering late that the company never supplied the required resume, reference, insurance item or technical answer. A missing-item register makes that work ownable while there is still time.

Use a bounded first job

Good Remedy’s starting price is $750 for a straightforward response up to 40 pages. That is a useful test when the company wants overflow capacity without committing to a full proposal team.

Hand off the work, not the judgment.

If the job is defined enough to operate, Good Remedy can often take the recurring queue or bounded backlog off your team.
