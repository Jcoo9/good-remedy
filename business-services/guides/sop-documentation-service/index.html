What should a useful SOP documentation service deliver?

A useful SOP lets someone who was not in the drafting conversation run the work, identify an exception and know who owns the decision. If it only describes the happy path, the organization still depends on tribal knowledge.

Published by Good Remedy · Business service guide

Capture the work that actually happens

Existing policy documents and training notes are useful, but the process usually lives across screens, forms, conversations and workarounds. The documentation step has to reconcile those sources rather than choosing whichever one looks most official.

Write the decisions, not only the clicks

“Open the file and update the system” is not enough when the operator has to decide which record is current, what counts as complete or when to escalate. Those decision points and exceptions are part of the procedure.

Keep regulated judgment with qualified owners

Healthcare, legal, safety, financial and technical procedures often contain decisions that a documentation service should not invent. The right output names the authoritative source or qualified reviewer and preserves that boundary.

Use the SOP as an operating test

Hand the draft to someone who did not help write it. Can they complete the job and recognize the situations that need escalation? Good Remedy’s launch package is $750 for up to five SOPs, including one revision round.

Hand off the work, not the judgment.

If the job is defined enough to operate, Good Remedy can often take the recurring queue or bounded backlog off your team.
