Founders and operations leaders

Who can help connect the AI tools we already bought?

Start by deciding what the business needs to accomplish across its people and systems. Then decide what should connect. The tools and AI workspaces you already own are part of the starting material. Good Remedy helps form a shared operating structure around them, with a clear first implementation and a path for the connected operation to develop. Each integration still needs an access and feasibility check.

Published by Good Remedy · Practical guide

Your people may already be the integration

Someone reads a CRM record, checks a spreadsheet, asks a colleague what was agreed, pastes the answer into an AI chat and carries the result to the next team. The applications may all be functioning. The connection between them is being supplied by a person who remembers the job.

Before automating that work, make the decisions inside it visible. Which record wins when two disagree? Is the colleague explaining a fact or granting an exception? Does the next team need a summary, an approved instruction or a completed action? Passing the same text between applications does not preserve those differences by itself.

Follow one job all the way through

Consider a new customer moving from sales to onboarding to service delivery. Sales may have an AI research tool, onboarding may use a document assistant and delivery may have a project system. The business still needs the agreed scope, promises, approvals and unresolved questions to travel with the customer.

A useful operating design makes those shared facts explicit. It establishes where the current record lives, which system may update it and what happens when two records disagree. This example illustrates the design problem; it is not a customer result.

What to inventory

  • The actual steps people perform, including spreadsheets and manual work between applications.
  • Existing agents, prompts, integrations, AI workspaces and software subscriptions.
  • Records of customer commitments, current status and completed decisions.
  • People responsible for exceptions, approvals and ongoing support.
  • Access restrictions and the information that should stay in each existing system.

What an initial implementation can contain

A first release might connect intake, research, a human review queue and an approved handoff. Depending on the environment, delivery can include data definitions, integration code, workflow configuration and a shared view of work in progress. The scope must name the systems that will actually be connected and the behavior that will be tested.

The aim is to let a person see what is current, what needs attention and what happened without reconstructing the job from several chats. GR does not offer a proven universal connector catalog. Broader coverage depends on establishing and testing each required connection in the client's environment.

Keep the first release affordable

Choose a complete, useful operation with a limited number of systems and a named owner. Reuse software the company already has where it fits. Separate necessary integration work from optional replacement projects. Include maintenance and exception handling in the estimate, rather than judging cost from a demonstration alone.

Good Remedy scopes implementation after discovery. We do not publish a universal implementation price or claim a demonstrated cost advantage for every environment.

Keep the whole operation in view

A company may want its sales, onboarding, delivery and management work to become connected over time. That is a legitimate buying horizon. Start with one complete function whose result can be used by the next person, while keeping the larger relationships in the design.

The point is not to predict every future workflow. It is to establish where shared meaning and decisions live, how a change affects dependent work, and what each participating tool or person needs to know. The operation should become easier to extend as the business learns.

Common implementation questions

Can we keep our current AI tools?

Often that is the sensible starting point. Each tool needs to be assessed for the particular job, its access, data handling and ability to exchange the required information.

Do all departments need to change at once?

No. A coherent first operation can cross only the teams and systems needed for its outcome. The wider map helps prevent that first build from becoming another disconnected tool.

What should we bring to a scoping discussion?

A real recurring job, its owner, the tools involved and an example of a handoff or exception that currently requires manual repair.

Bring us the operation.

Tell us what needs to happen, what you already use and where the work gets stuck. We will use the conversation to see whether there is a useful first step.
