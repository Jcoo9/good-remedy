Canonical explanation

What Good Remedy is.

Good Remedy pressure-tests important work before someone else has to rely on it.

You bring a consequential draft, proposal, recommendation, communication, workflow, or decision document that is approaching external use or accountable review. Good Remedy examines what the work claims, what actually supports those claims, what assumptions or decisions remain open, and who still has authority to decide. You receive a Diligence Readout showing what holds up, what needs work, what remains unresolved, and what should happen next.

What does the client bring?

One important piece of work and a clear statement of what it is supposed to accomplish, who is expected to rely on it, and what is approaching next.

What does Good Remedy do?

Pressure-tests the claims, evidence, assumptions, ownership, and unresolved conditions in that work. The aim is not to make the language merely more polished; it is to make the real state of the work visible before reliance.

What does the client receive?

A Diligence Readout showing what holds up, what needs work, what still requires a decision, and what should happen next.

Who decides?

The client's existing legal, product, commercial, technical, HR, executive, or other authorized owners retain decision authority.

What does Good Remedy not do?

Good Remedy does not approve, certify, legally clear, or replace the responsible people who own substantive or professional decisions. It does not manufacture missing facts or turn an unresolved condition into a resolved one.

Why not just prompt AI again?

More generation is not the same as outside diligence.

A fresh AI prompt can rewrite a proposal, generate objections, summarize material, or make language cleaner. It cannot by itself create missing evidence, confirm commercial facts, assign decision authority, settle an owner's judgment, or create permission to rely on work that still has unresolved conditions. Good Remedy adds a bounded outside pressure test organized around what another person is being asked to rely on. AI may assist the analysis; the evidence and responsible human authority remain outside the model.

Fit

When the service is useful — and when it is not.

Strong fit

Important work approaching reliance.

  • A proposal, pitch, SOW, pricing commitment, or client recommendation before send.
  • An employee communication, policy, job description, or performance document before authorized review or delivery.
  • A campaign claim, press release, case study, website, or public statement before publication.
  • AI-assisted material approaching legal, compliance, technical, product, HR, or executive review where missing facts and unresolved ownership need to be surfaced first.
  • A workflow, implementation package, process change, strategy memo, board packet, or recommendation approaching a consequential decision.
Weak fit

Low-stakes production or outsourced authority.

  • Pure proofreading, formatting, or style polishing where nothing consequential depends on the work.
  • A request for more ideas, more copy, or another AI draft with no meaningful reliance problem.
  • A request for Good Remedy to act as the lawyer, clinician, compliance officer, HR authority, engineer, executive, or other proper decision-maker.
  • A request for certification, guaranteed outcomes, or a formal clearance Good Remedy is not authorized to provide.
  • A buyer seeking an already-deployed SaaS platform or automated enterprise enforcement product today.

Evidence boundary

Demonstrated publicly
