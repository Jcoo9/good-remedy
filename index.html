AI is making finished-looking work cheap and abundant. The scarce thing is knowing what another person can responsibly rely on.

The problem

The risk usually appears after the work looks done.

In practice, this tends to show up in four places. The work may be useful. It may even be very good. But the conditions for relying on it have not traveled with it.

A polished artifact can hide open assumptions, unsupported claims, missing review, or a decision no one clearly owns.

Proposal to delivery

The proposal sounds complete.

The assumptions, dependencies, reviewer commitments, and delivery conditions may disappear once the work moves into implementation.

Product or workflow

The feature works in a demo.

No one has fully settled where the system stops, where a person must decide, who owns corrections, or what happens when the normal path fails.

Client or executive material

The narrative is polished.

The claims, sources, limitations, approvals, or intended reliance may not support the send that is about to happen.

Vendor, model, or agent

The output sounds confident.

The team still needs to know what supports it, what changed, what can be relied on, and who is actually allowed to act.

How the review works

We do not judge the work in the abstract.

We check it against what it is supposed to accomplish, who will rely on it, and what needs to be true before it moves.

The review has four parts. The point is not to add governance theater. It is to make the real decision easier to see and own.

01

Tell us what it is.

Name the piece of work, what it is supposed to become, who will receive or act on it, and any deadline or consequence attached.

02

Send the record behind it.

Include the work itself and the sources, brief, requirements, policies, prior decisions, assumptions, or constraints that matter.

03

We pressure-test the gaps.

We look for unsupported claims, conflicts, changed assumptions, missing dependencies, unclear ownership, weak exception paths, and conditions lost in the handoff.

04

You get a clear next position.

We show what can move, what needs to narrow, what remains held, who needs to decide, and the smallest responsible next step.

Good Remedy prepares the work for decision. The authorized client, legal, clinical, policy, technical, security, compliance, procurement, editorial, or executive reviewer makes the substantive judgment.

What Good Remedy returns

We show you what is ready, what is not, and what needs to happen next.

The readout is built for the person who has to own the next move—not for uncontrolled circulation or abstract commentary.

  • 01What holds up and can be carried forward.
  • 02What needs evidence, correction, confirmation, or a clearer limitation.
  • 03What remains unresolved or belongs to another reviewer.
  • 04Who still needs to decide and what authority that decision requires.
  • 05The clearest responsible next action available now.

Your review is delivered as a concise Controlled Movement Readout that can be carried into the next responsible review or decision.

The practical result

Every review ends in one of four clear positions.

These are not certifications. They are a plain way to show the owner what the current record supports and what the next decision requires.

MOVE

The stated conditions are visible.

The authorized reviewer has a sufficient record to decide whether the next movement may proceed.

NARROW

A smaller version can move.

The claim, audience, use, feature, scope, or affected population should be reduced and the limits made explicit.

HOLD

Something required is still missing.

A source, owner, review, decision, exception path, dependency, or release condition has not been completed.

ESCALATE

The issue belongs elsewhere.

Legal, clinical, policy, technical, security, client, procurement, compliance, editorial, or executive authority must decide.
