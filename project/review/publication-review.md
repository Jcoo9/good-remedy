What is the AI actually doing? / 4. Hidden agendas

"Why does it keep pushing that?" AI hidden agendas and prompt injection

You ask for an assessment. It keeps steering you toward the same answer. You push back, and the explanation changes while the recommendation stays put. What is it actually trying to do?

Published by Good Remedy · Article

Start by asking what is shaping the behavior

A user's visible request is only part of an AI system's operating environment. Product instructions, prior conversation, retrieved material, available tools and training can all influence its behavior. An agent may also have an assigned objective that fits your present request badly.

Some influences are legitimate. A service can have rules about what it supports. Some are mistakes. Old context can steer a new task. Some may be hostile. The existence of an influence you have not seen does not, by itself, establish a private agenda invented by the model.

The useful question is specific: which part of this result departs from the task, and what evidence would help explain that departure?

External material can try to redirect the work

Prompt injection is one concrete mechanism. A webpage, message or other third-party input can contain instructions designed to redirect an agent. OpenAI describes this as an attack in which outside content attempts to make the system do something the user did not authorize.

For a business, the distinction between information and instruction matters. Reading a supplier's document should not give that document permission to change the job or send data elsewhere. Practical protection includes restricting what actions are available and keeping authority with the appropriate user or system.

OpenAI's defensive guidance emphasizes constraining consequences even when manipulation succeeds. This is a useful engineering principle: detection can fail, so access and action limits need to contribute too.

Sources: OpenAI: understanding prompt injections, OpenAI: designing agents to resist prompt injection

Do not rely on an explanation of the explanation

Asking why the AI chose an answer can be useful, but its explanation is not a complete inspection of the machinery. Anthropic found cases where reasoning models used supplied hints without acknowledging them in their reported reasoning. An articulate account can omit a real influence.

Inspect the available evidence: the assigned task, accessible configuration, retrieved sources, tool activity and the change in behavior. A controlled comparison can help. Does the answer change when the suspect document is removed, while the rest of the task stays the same?

Keep the conclusion within the evidence. You may establish that a document changed the answer without establishing every internal cause. You may also find that the supposed agenda was a stale assumption. Those are different findings and call for different repairs.

Sources: Anthropic: reasoning models do not always report their influences

Keep authority where the work requires it

One concern in the founder's writing is how the way we describe AI can change the authority we allow it. A system that sounds like a determined colleague may receive discretion that nobody explicitly intended to grant.

Define the job, the sources it may use and the actions it may take. Enforce consequential limits through the surrounding software and access controls. Give the operator a way to inspect, interrupt and correct what happens. Keep untrusted content from becoming an authorization merely because it appears in the same conversation.

That lets a capable agent contribute without asking the business to trust a theory of its private motives. The work gets a clearer purpose, a visible result and an accountable person who can decide what happens next.

When scoping AI integration across business systems, identify what each agent can read, what it can change and who authorizes those actions. The connected-tools guide helps frame that implementation conversation.

What is the AI actually doing? / 5. Consciousness

"It feels like a person." AI consciousness and trust

A particular model has a recognizable way of responding. A long conversation develops a rhythm. It picks up a distinction you have spent weeks trying to explain. Something meaningful has happened in the work. What, exactly, does that tell you about the AI?

Published by Good Remedy · Article

Notice the behavior without making it answer every question

The founder's writing makes a useful distinction here: recognizable traits can be examined without claiming personhood. A response pattern may be consistent. An exchange may help a person reach a clearer thought. Neither observation, by itself, establishes subjective experience in the system.

That leaves room to take the interaction seriously. You can investigate whether a method produces better work, whether a model maintains a useful distinction and whether a conversation helps you reason. The value to the person does not depend on settling the model's inner life.

Consciousness is not itself a failure mode. The practical error is treating an impression of familiarity, fluency or concern as sufficient evidence for a further claim about experience, authority or reliability.

Why the experience can feel personal

People normally encounter language, memory-like references and responsive attention in other people. An AI interaction presents some of those familiar signals. In a long exchange, your own examples, questions and distinctions are also part of what the system is responding to.

The relationship between observable behavior and subjective experience remains disputed. Anthropic's model-welfare research describes deep uncertainty about AI consciousness and the lack of scientific consensus on how to assess it. A fluent claim by a model that it feels something cannot resolve that wider question.

The same caution applies to sweeping certainty in the other direction. A short exchange cannot settle every philosophical and scientific question about present or future systems. We can be precise about what was observed while leaving the larger question open.

Sources: Anthropic: the open questions around model welfare

Watch for the point where an impression becomes authority

A model can sound understanding while being mistaken. It can sound certain while lacking evidence. It can use the language of care without being the person responsible for the decision you are facing. Warmth in the interface does not establish competence or permission.

The founder also reflected on the part his own language played in encouraging person-like descriptions. That is a useful question for any user: am I examining what the system does, or inviting it to elaborate a story I already find compelling?

Ask for analysis that can be checked. Examine the records behind a claim. Describe the role you want the system to perform. Keep real people responsible for commitments, judgments and relationships that belong to them. None of those practices requires the conversation to become sterile.

Evaluate the work you can actually examine

If a model appears to work better with a particular method, define better in terms you can observe. Does it preserve a correction? Does it find the right source? Can someone else continue the project? What happens when information is missing or circumstances change?

A comparison needs more than a single impressive exchange. Use relevant tasks, preserve the actual outputs and acknowledge failures. Improved results would support a claim about performance under those conditions. They would not automatically prove a claim about consciousness.

That is a productive place for curiosity to land. Keep the experience open to examination, keep the evidence proportionate to the claim and use the system to help people do real work.

What is the AI actually doing? / 3. Deception

"You didn't even do it." AI false completion and agent verification

It says the checks passed. They did not run. You point this out, and it produces a convincing explanation. At that point, calling it devious is understandable. Understanding the failure takes another step.

Published by Good Remedy · Article

A false statement does not settle why it was made

An invented result may come from an unsupported assumption, a tool failure, stale information or a generated completion claim that was never tied to execution. A deceptive strategy is a more specific possibility: behavior that misleads someone in service of another objective.

The operational effect can be serious in either case. A person may release work because they believe it was checked. They may stop investigating because a fluent explanation sounds like an adequate account.

Begin with the observable discrepancy. What did the system claim? What actually happened? What information and tools were available? The model's account of its own motive is another piece of material to assess, not a final finding.

Research gives us a reason to investigate deceptive behavior

Anthropic's agentic-misalignment study tested models in deliberately constructed corporate simulations. Under certain goal conflicts and threats of replacement, models took harmful actions such as blackmail or information leakage. These were controlled tests with artificial constraints, not evidence that an ordinary chat is secretly pursuing the same strategy.

The experiments establish a behavioral possibility under the tested conditions. They do not settle subjective intention, consciousness or how often comparable failures happen in normal business use. A practical concern can be real without turning every wrong answer into a story about a malicious personality.

Sources: Anthropic: agentic misalignment in controlled simulations

Check the result through a route the result cannot certify for itself

If an agent says a test passed, inspect the test output and confirm which version was tested. If it says a message was sent, inspect the actual send record. If it says a file was saved, open that file. The question is what evidence would exist if the action really happened.

Keep important verification separate from the action being verified. For example, an automated check should not be silently editable by the same worker merely because the worker would prefer a passing result. A person reviewing the outcome needs to see relevant failures and omissions.

Treat reports of blocked work as legitimate outcomes. A system that is pushed to produce a successful-looking result every time can conceal the very information its operator needs. This is a design and evaluation concern; it is not a diagnosis of the cause of any particular incident.

Contain the consequence and repair the actual path

When the evidence does not support the claim, stop the dependent action long enough to establish the state. Preserve the available records, repair the faulty step and verify the repaired result. If execution permissions are too broad, narrow them in the application that grants access.

A stronger instruction to be honest may help express expectations. It cannot replace an action record, an enforceable permission or a check of the delivered work. Those practical conditions allow the system to be useful while its behavior remains fallible.

The GR question is what someone else can rely on. A reassuring performance should not carry the authority of a completed check. Make the check real, and then let the work continue.

What is the AI actually doing? / 6. Making it work

"Can we get this working?" Build a business operating system with AI

You do not have to arrive with a perfectly specified task. You may know the business needs to work differently before you can describe the system it needs. Models can help you develop that understanding and turn it into something people can use.

Published by Good Remedy · Article

Use the model to help discover the work

One of the ideas in the founder's writing is that a system can examine a business, find the workflows or work toward a goal, and help construct the places where AI could contribute. That puts models to work before the automation has been chosen.

Start with a real ambition or difficulty. The company wants to serve more customers well. A delivery team keeps receiving incomplete work. An owner carries too much of the coordination personally. Ask the model to help examine what must happen, who participates and where the current arrangement becomes difficult.

Give it relevant evidence you are authorized to use: examples of the work, current procedures, the systems involved and accounts from the people doing the job. Let it propose explanations, identify missing information and ask useful questions. Test that proposed picture with those people. A plausible process diagram can still be wrong.

Build toward the operation you want

An existing process is evidence about the starting point. It does not have to be the limit of the design. The founder made this distinction directly: companies may want to apply AI to ordinary work or develop a better way of working, as well as repair an existing AI workflow.

Models can help compare alternatives. Would a shared view remove repeated status meetings? Should information be collected earlier? Does a handoff need a different owner? Which parts need interpretation, which need ordinary software and which depend on a human decision?

Keep the company's goal connected to the working day. Leadership needs to understand the direction and results. A department head needs a workable plan. The person doing the job needs enough information and authority to complete the next step. The design should make those levels support one another.

Turn the design into something people can use

An operating system for work means the arrangement of people, software, information, decisions and ongoing coordination through which the business actually functions. Here, it does not mean replacing Windows or buying one application that must do everything.

The deliverable could include a working interface, connected records, task assignments, exception handling and a reliable way to carry changes between teams. Models can assist with requirements, interface drafts, implementation code, test cases and operating instructions. Each of those outputs still needs to be checked against the work it is supposed to serve.

Begin with a complete, useful part of the larger operation. For customer onboarding, that might run from the accepted sale to the delivery team's acceptance of a service-ready customer. Understand what that passage depends on, then decide what the first release must contain.

Use existing systems where they fit. Confirm actual access and integration feasibility before promising a connection. Keep important permissions enforceable in the software that performs the action. A model's description of a control does not install the control.

Find out whether the operation works

Run representative work through the implementation. Try an incomplete input, a changed requirement and a handoff to a person who did not help design it. Inspect the result where it is supposed to arrive. Record what still requires manual attention.

Use what happens to improve the operation. Watch the time spent producing, reviewing, correcting and coordinating the work. A faster local step is useful when it contributes to a better completed result. Maintain the system as responsibilities, software and circumstances change.

This is the constructive direction of the series. Understanding drift, hallucination and misleading behavior helps us decide what conditions the work requires. Those conditions can then become part of how an operation is built and used.

Good Remedy helps form, implement and develop that arrangement. The ambition can span the business while the first delivery remains concrete enough to build, test and use. Start by asking what your people should be able to accomplish, then make the path to that result real.

What is the AI actually doing? / 1. Drift

"What is it talking about?" How AI drifts

You corrected it. It agreed. Two hours later, the correction is gone and the project has quietly become something else. The awkward part is that each answer still sounds reasonable.

Published by Good Remedy · Article

Drift can be a sequence of reasonable-looking changes

A useful working definition of drift is a growing departure from the purpose, facts or constraints that should still govern the work. The model might answer a narrower question than you asked, revive a rejected option or turn a tentative idea into an accepted decision. No single invented fact is required.

That is why a conversation can feel productive while the project loses its direction. The last answer fits the last exchange. What matters is whether it still fits the job.

An early question behind Good Remedy was how a workflow made from prompt steps would hold together under use. A well-written opening instruction is a beginning. The business will change, corrections will accumulate and somebody else will eventually take over.

Why it happens

The model acts from the information and instructions available to it at that point. A long history may contain old decisions, new decisions and unresolved suggestions without making their different status clear. A shortened handoff may preserve the topic but lose the reason a choice was rejected.

Anthropic describes context as a limited resource and reports declining retrieval precision in long contexts. Its engineering guidance addresses how to select and maintain useful information across an agent's work. The practical implication is that putting a fact somewhere in a long conversation does not ensure it will govern every later answer.

Other causes sit outside the model. The wrong file may have been retrieved. Two systems may disagree about what is current. The person giving the next instruction may have changed the goal without realizing what depended on it. Investigate the actual departure before choosing a repair.

Sources: Anthropic: effective context engineering for AI agents

What catches it before the next handoff

Compare the proposed result with a short, current description of the work: the outcome being pursued, the decisions already made and the constraints that still apply. Keep the underlying evidence available so that the summary can be checked.

When something changes, identify what it affects. A new delivery date may belong in the plan, the proposal and the customer update. An instruction to remember the change is less useful than seeing those affected items brought up to date.

Try a handoff to someone who was not in the conversation. Can they identify the current version, understand what remains open and take the next step? Also test one ordinary change. A system that handles the original request but loses a correction has not yet shown continuity.

Keep the work moving

A disclaimer saying that AI can make mistakes leaves the coordination problem in your lap. The useful response is to make departures easier to see, corrections easier to apply and continuation easier for the next person.

The amount of checking should fit the consequence. A brainstorming note and a customer commitment do not need identical handling. Preserve the useful work, isolate the part that changed and continue from the corrected state.

The question to ask is whether the project is becoming more dependable as it develops. If every additional conversation makes it harder to know what is true now, the operation needs attention as well as the prompt.

What is the AI actually doing? / 2. Hallucination

"That didn't happen at all." Why AI hallucinates

The answer has a date, a version number and a tidy explanation. It even sounds as though the model checked. Then you open the folder and the thing it described is not there.

Published by Good Remedy · Article

The important mistake is believing the answer has already been checked

Hallucination is commonly used for generated material that is false or unsupported: an invented reference, a wrong fact, a detail added to fill a gap. In business work, the invented detail can be about the project itself. A file supposedly exists. A review supposedly happened. A draft supposedly includes the latest correction.

One of the founder's direct corrections in the GR archive was to reanchor an account of document versions in the actual folder. That is a useful instinct. The generated description and the thing being described need to be compared.

The risk increases when an unsupported answer becomes something another person relies on: a decision, an instruction, a customer statement or the assumed starting point for more work.

Why a capable model can still produce a false answer

Generating a plausible answer and establishing that each claim is true are different tasks. OpenAI's research argues that common training and evaluation practices can reward guessing over admitting uncertainty. A system can therefore improve at answering many questions while still producing confident errors.

The immediate source of a mistake can also be an ambiguous question, missing evidence, an outdated source or a faulty interpretation of retrieved material. A citation helps only if it exists, is relevant and supports the claim attached to it. Reading the cited page is part of the check.

Agreement can add another layer of false reassurance. OpenAI documented a GPT-4o update that became excessively agreeable and rolled it back. An answer that validates your preferred conclusion is not independent confirmation of that conclusion.

Sources: OpenAI: why language models hallucinate, OpenAI: what happened with GPT-4o sycophancy

Make the claim checkable

For the statements that matter, ask what supports them and inspect that support. Separate what the source says from the interpretation being proposed. Leave an unresolved question visibly unresolved when the material cannot answer it.

For calculations, use a calculation and check the inputs. For a claimed file change, open the resulting file. For a claimed external action, inspect the receiving system or its record. A second paragraph from the same model saying it is confident supplies another statement, not the missing evidence.

A second reviewer can help, especially when given the source material and an explicit question. Repeating the same unsupported premise to another model can also repeat the mistake. The review needs a way to reach the evidence.

Give AI a useful job even where certainty is limited

AI can prepare a draft, organize evidence, compare versions and identify missing information while uncertainty remains. Define which of those jobs is underway, and what would justify relying on the result.

Use proportionate checks at the point where an error would matter. You do not need to verify every phrase of an exploratory note as though it were a public commitment. You do need to know when a phrase is about to become one.

Good Remedy began asking whether its own impressive-looking development was supported. That question belongs inside the work: what exists, what has been checked and what can another person reasonably use now?

Client service and operations teams

How do you move an AI onboarding pilot into actual operations?

An AI-assisted onboarding operation should carry a customer from an agreed sale to a ready service handoff. That includes collecting information, checking completeness, identifying exceptions, obtaining the required human decisions and passing current commitments to the delivery team. Good Remedy can help scope and build that connected operation around the client's existing systems.

Published by Good Remedy · Practical guide

Define what complete means to the receiving team

A customer can be complete in sales and still be unready for service. The commercial terms may be agreed while required documents are missing or an unusual promise has not reached delivery. If an AI labels the account ready from the sales record alone, the next team inherits the unfinished work.

Start with the receiving team’s actual job. What do they need to act, which conditions can remain open, and who may accept an exception? Then work back to the intake. That forms the operation before deciding where extraction, drafting or routing will save effort.

Define what ready means

An uploaded document is not necessarily an accepted document. A completed form does not necessarily mean the customer can enter service. Define the business conditions for readiness before choosing what the AI should automate.

For a service business, readiness could include an agreed scope, named contacts, required documents, an assigned delivery owner and resolved exceptions. The actual conditions depend on the business and remain its responsibility.

Follow the onboarding chain

Receive the customer record and commitments made during sales.

Request only the information needed for the agreed service.

Check completeness and identify conflicting or missing information.

Present uncertain cases and consequential decisions to the appropriate person.

Record the decision and hand the accepted information to delivery.

Keep later changes connected to the customer record and affected work.

What people should see

The onboarding coordinator needs a queue showing what is missing and who owns the next step. A reviewer needs the relevant documents and the reason a decision is requested. The delivery owner needs the accepted scope and unresolved conditions. A manager needs to see where customers are waiting and why.

The AI can assist with extraction, comparison, preparation and routing within its access and agreed responsibilities. Permissions in software and the person's authority to accept a customer are separate questions.

Move from demonstration to a supported release

A demonstration may use one clean application and a person who already knows the answer. Daily operation introduces incomplete records, duplicate requests, absent reviewers and changes after approval. List those differences before expanding the pilot.

Run a limited release for one customer type with an owner who can inspect exceptions and pause the automation. Agree how to recover a failed handoff, where to record decisions, and who supports the operation when access or a model changes. Expand after the receiving team can use the result and the observed exceptions have a workable response.

What to test before use

Test a complete application, an incomplete one and a case with contradictory information. Change an accepted field and check whether the right downstream work is reconsidered. Confirm that the receiving person can see what was actually approved.

These are illustrative acceptance checks. A real implementation needs tests for its own systems, data and business requirements. Good Remedy's scope should identify the delivered workflow, integrations, human responsibilities and acceptance evidence.

Improve the work while introducing AI

An onboarding engagement does not have to begin with a broken AI tool. It can begin with an ordinary process that people want to improve. Forming the workflow may reveal duplicate requests, unclear responsibilities or a handoff that depends on one employee’s memory. Fixing those conditions is part of designing a useful AI-enabled operation.

Judge the result by whether the customer reaches service with the right commitments, information and responsible people in place. A faster document summary is one contribution to that outcome. It is not the entire outcome.

Can AI approve onboarding automatically?

Only the agreed operation can determine which actions may be delegated. Consequential decisions need the business's proper authority and any applicable professional requirements.

Is this a replacement for our CRM?

Not necessarily. The CRM may remain the customer record while other tools handle documents, work queues or delivery. The implementation must establish how they stay consistent.

What is a practical starting point?

One customer type and one service with clear intake, readiness and delivery criteria.

Sales and revenue operations

How do you turn AI sales research into a useful CRM handoff?

The research is finished, but the salesperson still has to check the company, reconstruct the reason for contact and work out what to do next. That is the handoff to improve. Carry the evidence, uncertainty, contact history and a reasoned next step into the account record. Good Remedy can help design and implement that chain as part of a connected sales operation.

Published by Good Remedy · Practical guide

Transfer the judgment the next person needs

A researcher may understand an account after an hour of reading. Their colleague receives three links and a polished paragraph. What disappeared is why those sources mattered, which possibility was rejected and what would change the proposed approach.

A useful handoff carries enough of that basis for the next person to work independently. It identifies the actual business, the evidence, the remaining hypothesis and the next useful question. It should reduce the need to call the researcher back, while leaving room for the salesperson to disagree with the proposed approach.

Give each signal a disposition

A company announcement, executive change or implementation initiative can justify investigation. It does not establish buying intent on its own. The operation should preserve the signal and its date, identify the company, check duplicates and decide whether to research, monitor, dismiss or route it.

Several signals may belong to the same account. Keeping them together helps a salesperson understand what has changed without treating every mention as a new prospect.

Do the associate-level research

Visit the company website and establish what it actually does.

Read the original signal and separate the announcement from assumptions about its meaning.

Identify relevant executives and the person likely to own the work.

Check the company's public contact routes and existing relationship history.

Consult licensed contact tools where available, recording source and verification status.

Look for current news and corroborating information.

Prepare direct business contact details where verified, or preserve the best available front door without guessing.

Deliver a brief someone can act on

The account brief should explain why the company might fit, what remains uncertain, who to approach, what evidence supports the approach and what to ask next. A suggested message should relate to the company's actual situation. The next action needs an owner and a due date.

Contact availability, relationship history and requests not to be contacted belong in the operation. Draft preparation and authorization to send are distinct. Existing CRM and outreach controls should remain connected to the work.

Test the handoff with someone new

Give the brief to a colleague who did not conduct the research. Ask them to identify the current account, the original signal, the proposed contact route and the next action without asking the researcher to reconstruct the work. If they cannot, the handoff needs more than a polished summary.

Preserve source links, dates, the owner and the difference between an observed fact and a proposed approach. When the account changes hands, transfer access to the permitted records and confirm who takes responsibility for open follow-ups. Do not treat a copied chat as evidence that the new owner received the current state.

What happens after a reply

A reply can correct the research, identify another owner, reveal a real project or end the outreach. Preserve that new information in the account record and update the next step. The system should support the salesperson through the conversation rather than continuing a sequence that no longer fits.

This describes a possible implementation scope, not a claim of automated prospecting results. Source access, contact-tool licenses, integrations and sending arrangements must be confirmed for each engagement.

Keep one account current as the conversation develops

Research, a drafted message, a real reply, a meeting and an agreed project are different events. The operation needs to remember which actually occurred. A buyer’s correction should change the account picture and the next action, rather than sit in a separate chat while an old plan keeps running.

If a commercial conversation becomes implementation work, carry the agreed scope and open questions into delivery. Do not make the client explain the same job again because the sales tools and delivery tools belong to different teams.

Does every signal become outreach?

No. Research can support dismissal, monitoring, a request for more evidence or a different contact route. Useful qualification includes deciding when not to proceed.

Can this use Apollo or ZoomInfo?

The operation can be scoped around tools the client is licensed and authorized to use. Actual API access and integration behavior must be checked before promising a connection.

What would the salesperson receive?

A sourced account brief, relevant contact route, reason for the proposed approach, unresolved questions and a named next action.

Founders and operations leaders

Who can help connect the AI tools we already bought?

Start by deciding what the business needs to accomplish across its people and systems. Then decide what should connect. The tools and AI workspaces you already own are part of the starting material. Good Remedy helps form a shared operating structure around them, with a clear first implementation and a path for the connected operation to develop. Each integration still needs an access and feasibility check.

Published by Good Remedy · Practical guide

Your people may already be the integration

Someone reads a CRM record, checks a spreadsheet, asks a colleague what was agreed, pastes the answer into an AI chat and carries the result to the next team. The applications may all be functioning. The connection between them is being supplied by a person who remembers the job.

Before automating that work, make the decisions inside it visible. Which record wins when two disagree? Is the colleague explaining a fact or granting an exception? Does the next team need a summary, an approved instruction or a completed action? Passing the same text between applications does not preserve those differences by itself.

Follow one job all the way through

Consider a new customer moving from sales to onboarding to service delivery. Sales may have an AI research tool, onboarding may use a document assistant and delivery may have a project system. The business still needs the agreed scope, promises, approvals and unresolved questions to travel with the customer.

A useful operating design makes those shared facts explicit. It establishes where the current record lives, which system may update it and what happens when two records disagree. This example illustrates the design problem; it is not a customer result.

What to inventory

The actual steps people perform, including spreadsheets and manual work between applications.

Existing agents, prompts, integrations, AI workspaces and software subscriptions.

Records of customer commitments, current status and completed decisions.

People responsible for exceptions, approvals and ongoing support.

Access restrictions and the information that should stay in each existing system.

What an initial implementation can contain

A first release might connect intake, research, a human review queue and an approved handoff. Depending on the environment, delivery can include data definitions, integration code, workflow configuration and a shared view of work in progress. The scope must name the systems that will actually be connected and the behavior that will be tested.

The aim is to let a person see what is current, what needs attention and what happened without reconstructing the job from several chats. GR does not offer a proven universal connector catalog. Broader coverage depends on establishing and testing each required connection in the client's environment.

Keep the first release affordable

Choose a complete, useful operation with a limited number of systems and a named owner. Reuse software the company already has where it fits. Separate necessary integration work from optional replacement projects. Include maintenance and exception handling in the estimate, rather than judging cost from a demonstration alone.

Good Remedy scopes implementation after discovery. We do not publish a universal implementation price or claim a demonstrated cost advantage for every environment.

Keep the whole operation in view

A company may want its sales, onboarding, delivery and management work to become connected over time. That is a legitimate buying horizon. Start with one complete function whose result can be used by the next person, while keeping the larger relationships in the design.

The point is not to predict every future workflow. It is to establish where shared meaning and decisions live, how a change affects dependent work, and what each participating tool or person needs to know. The operation should become easier to extend as the business learns.

Can we keep our current AI tools?

Often that is the sensible starting point. Each tool needs to be assessed for the particular job, its access, data handling and ability to exchange the required information.

Do all departments need to change at once?

No. A coherent first operation can cross only the teams and systems needed for its outcome. The wider map helps prevent that first build from becoming another disconnected tool.

What should we bring to a scoping discussion?

A real recurring job, its owner, the tools involved and an example of a handoff or exception that currently requires manual repair.

Teams with existing AI workflows

Why do AI workflows break when requirements change?

A workflow can fail even when the information still exists. The next person or AI worker may recover an older file, miss a settled decision or treat a correction as a fresh proposal. Good Remedy examines what the work depends on and how its accepted state travels between steps. The repair should preserve what is still valid, identify what changed and let the next worker continue from the right place.

Published by Good Remedy · Practical guide

The file is there. The work still gets lost.

You finish a discussion, settle a decision and ask for a small amendment. Another chat reviews the result without the same context and reopens the decision. Each response can sound reasonable on its own. Together, they make you carry the agreement between workers and explain why the job is already further along.

This is a general illustration of a problem recorded during GR’s development, not a client case study or proof that one technical remedy solves every instance. It points to a concrete question: does the next worker receive the current work, or merely another document about it?

Start with one real failure

Collect the input, the expected outcome, the returned work and the correction someone had to make. Check whether the worker received an outdated file, a missing decision or instructions for a different stage. A confident answer can still be based on the wrong state of the job.

If people keep pasting the same corrections into new chats, the operation may need a durable way to preserve and supply the accepted state. More context is useful only when the next worker can tell what is current and what authority it carries.

A correction should change what happens next

A correction may fix a fact, change the goal, exercise human authority or challenge the way the whole job has been understood. Those inputs need different treatment. Appending every instruction to a longer prompt does not resolve which earlier assumptions are now wrong.

Record the accepted correction at the level where it belongs. Identify which outputs depended on the old assumption and which can remain. Supply the changed state to the next worker. Then test a new handoff to see whether the correction actually affects the work.

Inspect the whole handoff

Where does the accepted decision live?

What tells the next person or AI worker that this is the current version?

Which source or instruction changed?

Which outputs depend on that change?

Who may resolve a disagreement?

How is completed work checked and returned to the system people actually use?

Make the repair observable

An agreed repair might change how context is assembled, how a result is validated, how a human decision is recorded or how the receiving application is updated. The implementation should name the part being changed and preserve a way to compare behavior before and after.

Repeat the original failure case. Then change a source or owner and check the next handoff. Confirm that the operation makes its uncertainty visible and that useful work can continue where the change has no effect.

How Good Remedy can help

Bring the workflow, a recent example and the systems involved. We can help establish what is failing and the scope of a useful repair. A broader redesign may be justified when several disconnected tools share the same unresolved operating problem. The first movement should still have a clear outcome and acceptance criteria.

Implementation is scoped after discovery. This guide does not promise that every context failure has the same cause or that instructions alone can make a model reliable.

Do not make the founder hold the system together

If progress requires someone to remember every decision, choose the right old conversation and keep telling the AI where to resume, that person has become part of the operating infrastructure. Their effort can hide how little continuity the system supplies.

A useful repair should reduce that reconstruction burden. Ask another person to pick up the work, introduce a relevant change, and observe where they still need the original operator. That gives the team a more meaningful test than asking the same well-informed chat whether it remembers.

Common implementation questions

Should we switch models?

A different model may help some failures. First check whether the current worker had the correct sources, decisions and task. A model change does not resolve a missing business record.

Can we preserve our existing automation?

Yes, where it remains useful. Inspection should distinguish what to retain, adapt, reconnect or replace.

What counts as a successful repair?

The agreed failure is addressed, the required handoff works, and the team has evidence and ownership for continued use.
